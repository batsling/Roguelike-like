## How it was made

Every Steam game tagged Roguelike, Roguelite, Action Roguelike, Roguelike Deckbuilder or Traditional Roguelike with **100+ reviews** (the English-review count Steam's search shows): **2,960**, of which **2,193** are not on the chart.

- **Inspiration finds** (part 1): each game's store text and developer announcements were matched with `tools/influence_research.py`'s own claim words and chart-name patterns (native-script titles included). 236 games had a sentence naming a chart game; after reading them all, the ones listed are the developer's own words about this game. Rogueliker's 52 developer interviews and inspiration posts were all read: three new games (Mortal Glory 2, Sirocco, RIFTSTORM). Nine more came from interviews and forum posts found by hand, each checked at the source: EVERSPACE, NEO Scavenger, Jump Space, Mullet MadJack, Cuisineer, In Death, GetsuFumaDen: Undying Moon and Killing Room, plus Descenders, whose interview page refuses this machine (its entry says so).
- **Deeper passes** over the 1,427 roguelikes and borderline games with no find: the research tool's studio-site and press-kit pass, its itch.io pass and its Bluesky pass, run over these games (every hit read, and each Bluesky poster checked against the game's Steam developer), plus a pass over the Chinese, Japanese and Korean versions of the Steam pages of the 106 games whose title or developer is written in those scripts. Bluesky gave six, studio sites five, the native-language pages one (Night of Full Moon), itch none.
- **Wikipedia:** Wikidata's Steam-app links name the English Wikipedia article of 246 of the off-chart games. Each was scanned the same way and every hit followed to the interview it cites, since Wikipedia is second-hand: seven held up (Marvel's Midnight Suns, Tribes of Midgard, TOKOYO, Into the Stars, GALAK-Z, Little Noah, Towns); the rest were already listed, comparisons, or a citation that doesn't say it. The 202 games with an article in another language gave two more, FATE (from its French article) and Feed the Scorchpot (Czech). The same pages in other languages, kept only where really written in that language: Russian (657) gave two weaker lines, Envy the Dead and Neurodeck; French (608) two, Pixel Starships and Book Shooter; Polish (328) and German (498) nothing new. Looking the other 1,326 games up by title found 11 more articles and nothing in them. Wikidata's own "inspired by" statements gave two more (PROJECT TACHYON, Towa), each checked at the reference it carries.
- **Matching fixes** found along the way, all in `tools/influence_research.py`: pitch lines that say "blends", "combines" or "a fusion of" weren't read as claims (thirteen games' store pages, announcements and press kits, all now in part 1 as weaker); bracketed names ("[Dream Quest]") were stripped as markup; a curly apostrophe ("Don’t Starve") didn't match the sheet's straight one; the Japanese claim words lacked インスピレーション; and Japanese names came only from Steam's localised titles (now Wikidata's labels too, with katakana names kept whole). Re-matching the cached text gave Night of Full Moon, Niche and TOKOYO.
- **Same-developer finds** (part 2): each game's developer, from its store page, matched exactly (ignoring Inc., Ltd. and the like) against the developers of chart games. Editions of chart games (prologues, demos, ports, remasters) are left out.
- **Roguelike?** is where a rogue tag sits in the game's own Steam tags, ranked by votes. Calibrated on 79 chart games: 75 of them have one in their top 6, so *yes* = top 6, *borderline* = 7th–11th, *no* = lower or none.

The scripts were one-offs and are not in the repo.
